⏺ Lighthouses began in antiquity, when fires burned atop headlands to warn mariners of danger. The Pharos of
Alexandria, built around 280 BCE, became one of the ancient world's wonders, guiding ships safely for centuries. Romans
erected towers across their empire; the Tower of Hercules in Spain still operates today. Medieval Europe entrusted many
lights to monks and guilds, burning wood or coal in open braziers. The eighteenth century brought sturdier engineering,
notably John Smeaton's Eddystone tower, which used interlocking stone. Argand lamps and parabolic reflectors improved
brightness, then Augustin-Jean Fresnel's stepped lens of 1822 transformed visibility, projecting beams far offshore.
Electrification followed, and automation gradually replaced keepers. Today satellite navigation has diminished their
role, yet lighthouses endure as cherished landmarks.
