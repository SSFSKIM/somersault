⏺ The typewriter's history stretches back to the late eighteenth century, with
  early patents by inventors like Henry Mill in 1714, though practical machines
  did not emerge until the 1800s. The Sholes and Glidden typewriter, developed
  by Christopher Latham Sholes and introduced commercially in 1874, became the
  first widely produced model and established the QWERTY keyboard layout still
  used today. Throughout the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries,
  typewriters transformed offices, journalism, and literature, enabling faster
  written communication and opening clerical work to women. Manufacturers such
  as Remington, Underwood, and Royal competed fiercely, refining mechanisms like
  shift keys and ribbon systems. Electric typewriters emerged mid-century,
  notably IBM's Selectric in 1961, before word processors and personal computers
  gradually rendered the typewriter obsolete by the late twentieth century,
  though it remains a cultural icon.
